# Keystone Uncertainty

Use this reference for all research tasks.

## Definition

The keystone uncertainty is the smallest researchable question that carries the most decision weight.

It is not necessarily:
- the broadest question
- the most interesting question
- the question with the most available data
- the question the user first asked
- the question that produces the most complete report

It is the question that, if answered or partially killed, would most reduce uncertainty about the decision.

## Selection criteria

A strong keystone uncertainty is:
1. decision-dominant: it determines whether the decision works
2. uncertain: it is not already settled by common knowledge or obvious facts
3. researchable: at least a cheap first test can be run
4. falsifiable: some evidence could weaken or kill it
5. upstream: many later questions become less important if it fails
6. bounded: it can be tested without researching the whole world

## Selection procedure

Before listing many hypotheses, generate 3 to 5 candidate uncertainties and score them.

Use this table:

| candidate uncertainty | decision weight | uncertainty | researchability | falsifiability | upstream leverage | priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Pick the candidate with the best combination of high decision weight, high uncertainty, and cheap testability. Do not pick a question merely because it is easy to answer.

## Common patterns

### Investment research
Prefer questions such as:
- What expectation is already embedded in price?
- What must be true for earnings, cash flow, margins, or multiples to exceed that embedded expectation?
- What mechanism would sustain the required outcome?
- What signal would show the mechanism is breaking?

Avoid starting with:
- Is the company good?
- Is the industry attractive?
- What is the TAM?
- What are the latest news items?

### Strategy research
Prefer questions such as:
- Which assumption determines whether this strategic path is worth pursuing?
- What must be true about customer behavior, distribution, economics, or competition?
- What cheap test could show the assumption is false?

Avoid starting with broad market maps unless the decision cannot yet be scoped.

### Product research
Prefer questions such as:
- What user behavior must change for this product bet to work?
- Which adoption barrier would kill the product thesis?
- What is the cheapest observable behavior that tests the barrier?

### Abstract or future inquiry
Prefer questions such as:
- Which assumption carries the scenario?
- Which causal link is most fragile?
- Which local claim is testable today?
- What monitoring signal would force an update?

## Failure modes

Avoid these mistakes:
- creating a broad hypothesis map before selecting the keystone uncertainty
- treating the user's first question as automatically keystone
- choosing a question because it is easy, not because it is decision-dominant
- escalating to interviews or deep research before running a cheap kill test
- covering background material that does not affect the keystone uncertainty
- confusing a plausible narrative with a testable uncertainty
