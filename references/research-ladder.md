# Research Ladder

Use this ladder after selecting the keystone uncertainty. The ladder is not a menu of increasingly sophisticated work. It is a kill ladder: each level should test a specific link in the what-has-to-be-true chain.

## Level 0: keystone selection and chain map

Best for: all research tasks.

Typical moves:
- restate the decision
- identify candidate uncertainties
- select the keystone uncertainty
- write the what-has-to-be-true chain
- define the first cheap kill test

Cost: very low
Time: 10 to 30 minutes
Evidence quality: weak directional evidence, but high leverage for focus

Output:
- keystone uncertainty
- chain links
- kill / preserve / escalate criteria
- what to postpone

## Level 1: embedded-expectation or base-rate test

Best for: checking whether the thesis already requires extreme assumptions.

Typical moves:
- reverse-engineer market, user, customer, or organizational expectations
- compare to base rates or historical precedent
- check obvious contradictions in primary sources
- identify the most sensitive variable

Examples:
- investment: reverse-engineer the earnings or cycle duration implied by price
- product: define what adoption behavior must occur for the product to work
- strategy: define what market or capability assumption must be true

Cost: low
Time: 30 minutes to 3 hours
Evidence quality: weak to moderate

Kill when:
- the thesis requires extreme assumptions without a plausible mechanism
- obvious evidence already contradicts the key assumption

Preserve when:
- the implied assumption is modest or plausibly wrong
- no cheap evidence contradicts the key link

Escalate when:
- the assumption remains decisive but evidence is incomplete or conflicting

## Level 2: mechanism and rival-mechanism test

Best for: testing whether the required outcome can be sustained.

Typical moves:
- map the mechanism that must sustain the outcome
- map at least one rival mechanism
- identify evidence that discriminates between them
- check the weakest link first

Cost: low to medium
Time: 1 to 6 hours
Evidence quality: weak to moderate

Kill when:
- a required mechanism link fails
- a rival mechanism explains the evidence better

Preserve when:
- the mechanism remains plausible and the rival is weaker

Escalate when:
- the key link depends on hidden facts, expert knowledge, or direct observation

## Level 3: source-grounded desk scan or artifact deep dive

Best for: collecting direct evidence for a specific unresolved chain link.

Typical moves:
- read primary documents
- extract fields into a claim table
- compare recent data to historical data
- inspect artifacts, pricing, contracts, releases, reviews, filings, or transcripts
- tag facts, claims, inferences, assumptions, and speculation

Cost: low to medium
Time: several hours to 2 days
Evidence quality: moderate when sources are credible and audit trail is clear

Kill when:
- primary evidence contradicts the keystone assumption
- the source record shows the thesis is already stale or overclaimed

Preserve when:
- primary evidence supports the key link with limits

Escalate when:
- public or available documents cannot resolve the decisive link

## Level 4: structured comparison, database analysis, or quantitative sensitivity

Best for: testing whether a pattern holds across cases, time, or scenarios.

Typical moves:
- structured sample comparison
- historical base-rate analysis
- quantitative sensitivity model
- database extraction
- scenario comparison
- counterexample search

Cost: medium
Time: 1 to 5 days
Evidence quality: moderate, sometimes strong when data quality is high

Kill when:
- the thesis fails under reasonable assumptions
- comparison cases show the proposed mechanism is not distinctive
- sensitivity depends on one fragile variable

Preserve when:
- multiple checks point in the same direction and counterexamples are limited

Escalate when:
- data remains inconclusive but the decision value justifies primary research

## Level 5: primary validation or expert challenge

Best for: hidden facts, operational mechanisms, customer behavior, private process, or causal links that cannot be resolved from available sources.

Typical moves:
- targeted expert calls
- customer or user interviews
- operator interviews
- field tests
- experiments
- private data analysis
- adversarial review by domain specialists

Cost: medium to high
Time: days to weeks
Evidence quality: moderate to strong depending on respondent quality, independence, and design

Kill when:
- high-quality primary evidence contradicts the key mechanism

Preserve when:
- independent sources support the key mechanism and limits are understood

Escalate when:
- decision is high-stakes, evidence remains split, and additional work could still change the decision

## Choosing levels

Start at the lowest level that can materially reduce the keystone uncertainty.

Do not escalate because:
- the topic is broad
- the topic is interesting
- a complete report would look better
- a heavier method sounds more rigorous

Escalate only because:
- the keystone uncertainty survived the cheap test
- the unresolved link remains decision-dominant
- the next method tests that exact unresolved link
- the decision value justifies the cost
