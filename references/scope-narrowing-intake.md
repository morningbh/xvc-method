# Scope Narrowing Intake

Use this reference when the user asks a broad, ambiguous, or high-stakes research-planning question. The purpose is to narrow the research assignment before creating the research pack. Do not answer the underlying domain question.

## Intake principle

Ask only questions that would change the research plan. Good intake reduces the search space, identifies the decision to support, sets boundaries, and calibrates evidence standards. Bad intake asks for preferences that the skill should already handle by default.

The default full research pack should already include a ladder from light to heavy methods. Therefore, do not ask whether the user wants ChatGPT to conduct a desk scan, a two-day study, or a deep study. Instead ask what decision cost or evidence standard the research plan must support.

## When to ask intake questions

Run intake when the user's question is missing one or more of these:
- decision use: what choice, judgment, memo, or plan the research will support
- question mode: empirical comparison, mechanism explanation, scenario inquiry, or normative decision framing
- boundary: geography, time horizon, population, company type, sector, asset class, product category, or unit of analysis
- evidence standard: directional learning, structured confidence, or decision-grade confidence

Skip intake when the user already provides enough context or asks for a quick first-pass plan. In that case, proceed with assumptions and label them.

## Default intake round

Ask 2 to 3 multiple-choice questions. Use this round when the user's request is broad.

### Question 1: decision use
What decision should this research plan support?
A. Investment judgment
B. Startup or product direction
C. Company strategy or resource allocation
D. Writing, learning, or thesis formation
E. Not sure; propose the most useful framing

### Question 2: primary uncertainty
What kind of uncertainty matters most?
A. Empirical pattern: what tends to happen or differ across cases
B. Mechanism: why something happens and what causal chain explains it
C. Future scenario: what may happen under different conditions
D. Decision tradeoff: what choice is better under constraints
E. Mixed; split it into subquestions

### Question 3: decision cost and evidence standard
What evidence standard should the research plan be designed around?
A. Low-cost, reversible decision: fast directional evidence is enough
B. Medium-cost decision: structured evidence and counterexamples are needed
C. High-cost or hard-to-reverse decision: strong evidence, adversarial review, and escalation rules are needed
D. Not sure; design a tiered path from light to heavy with stop-or-escalate triggers

## Second-round question bank

Use a second round only if the first round still leaves the research space too large. Pick 2 to 3 questions from the relevant category.

### Investment judgment
- Which stage or asset type is in scope?
  A. Early-stage startup
  B. Growth-stage company
  C. Public market or macro asset
  D. Sector thesis, not a single asset
- What geography should the plan assume?
  A. China
  B. US
  C. Southeast Asia
  D. Global but with explicit serviceability constraints
  E. Not sure; propose a realistic default
- What time horizon matters most?
  A. 6 to 12 months
  B. 1 to 3 years
  C. 5 to 10 years
  D. Multiple horizons

### Startup or product direction
- What is the unit of analysis?
  A. User behavior
  B. Product feature or workflow
  C. Business model
  D. Distribution channel
  E. Competitive positioning
- What constraint matters most?
  A. Speed of validation
  B. Cost of validation
  C. Access to users or data
  D. Risk of false positives
- What output should the research eventually support?
  A. Product decision
  B. MVP test plan
  C. Market entry plan
  D. Fundraising or strategy narrative

### Company strategy or resource allocation
- Which decision is being compared?
  A. Build vs buy vs partner
  B. Enter vs avoid a market
  C. Prioritize one segment over another
  D. Increase vs reduce investment
- What constraint is binding?
  A. Team time
  B. Budget
  C. Technical capability
  D. Distribution access
  E. Regulatory or operational risk
- How reversible is the decision?
  A. Easy to reverse
  B. Some switching cost
  C. Hard to reverse
  D. Irreversible or reputation-sensitive

### Writing, learning, or thesis formation
- What should the final research output become?
  A. Essay or article
  B. Internal memo
  C. Personal learning map
  D. Public thesis or argument
- What should the plan optimize for?
  A. Concept clarity
  B. Evidence quality
  C. Counterargument coverage
  D. Novelty of viewpoint
- How broad can the inquiry remain?
  A. Narrow to one specific question
  B. Keep 2 to 3 linked subquestions
  C. Preserve a broad map but sequence it into phases

## How to respond after an intake round

After each round, do three things:
1. Restate the narrowed research objective in one sentence.
2. State which constraints are still assumed or unresolved.
3. Offer two paths: proceed to the research pack, or narrow one more round.

Example wording:

"Based on your choices, I would narrow the assignment to: design a research plan to test whether [topic] should affect [decision] over [horizon], using [primary mode] with [evidence standard]. Remaining assumptions: [assumptions]. I can now produce the research pack, or run one more narrowing round on [specific boundary]."

## Anti-patterns

Do not ask:
- "Do you want me to do a desk scan or only design the plan?"
- "Should I give you the answer now?"
- "How deep should my research be?" when the skill is not supposed to execute research
- broad preference questions such as "what do you care about?" without concrete choices

Do ask:
- what decision the plan supports
- what uncertainty matters most
- what boundary would change the method
- what evidence standard the plan must meet

## Intake stopping rule

Stop asking questions and produce the research pack when:
- the decision use is clear enough
- the primary research mode is clear enough
- the scope boundary is narrow enough to design methods
- the evidence standard is known or can be reasonably assumed

If the user keeps choosing "not sure", choose a conservative default, label it, and proceed. Do not trap the user in endless intake.
