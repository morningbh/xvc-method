# AI-Assisted Research

Use this reference when AI tools can accelerate research planning or execution. AI tools include ChatGPT, Claude, DeepSeek, Perplexity, Deep Research, search agents, internal assistants, and similar systems.

## Core boundary

AI tools are research accelerators, not evidence by default.

Use AI outputs for:
- scoping
- hypothesis generation
- rival explanations
- source mapping
- search-query design
- extraction schemas
- summarizing provided sources
- clustering and comparison
- adversarial critique
- synthesis audit
- prompt generation

Do not treat AI outputs as evidence unless claims are tied to cited, checkable, independent sources or primary data.

## Where AI fits in the research plan

### Phase 0: scope and prompt rewrite
Use AI to convert a broad question plus intake answers into a precise research assignment.

Output:
- research objective
- decision context
- boundaries
- subquestions
- evidence standard

### Phase 1: hypothesis and source strategy
Use AI to generate hypotheses, null views, rival mechanisms, source categories, search queries, and inclusion/exclusion criteria.

Output:
- hypothesis map
- source map
- search plan
- extraction fields

### Phase 2: evidence extraction
Use AI to structure and extract evidence from sources supplied by the researcher.

Output:
- claim table
- source/date
- evidence type
- support and limitation
- fact/inference/speculation split

### Phase 3: adversarial critique
Use AI to stress-test assumptions, identify counterexamples, detect source bias, and propose falsification tests.

Output:
- fragile assumption list
- rival explanations
- missing evidence
- escalation recommendation

### Phase 4: synthesis audit
Use AI to check whether the eventual research synthesis overclaims, ignores counterevidence, or blurs speculation and evidence.

Output:
- overclaim checks
- confidence calibration
- source-audit checklist
- unresolved uncertainties

## Multi-model use

Use multiple models for role diversity, not voting.

Good patterns:
- ChatGPT or Deep Research for source-grounded multi-step research planning or cited synthesis
- another model for adversarial critique
- another model for Chinese-language context, local search terms, or alternative framing
- a search-oriented tool for source discovery

Do not write fixed claims that one model is always best. Model capabilities change. Assign roles by task and require auditability.

If models disagree, inspect:
- definitions
- hidden assumptions
- source sets
- time windows
- whether one model introduced unsupported claims

## Prompt design rules

Every prompt generated by this skill should:
- state the research objective and decision context
- define the phase of work
- prohibit premature final answers when the phase is planning, extraction, or critique
- require separation of facts, inferences, assumptions, and speculation
- require sources or state when no source is available
- ask for missing evidence and uncertainty
- produce a structured output that can be audited

## Prompt templates

### 1. Scope refinement prompt

```text
You are helping design a research plan, not answer the research question.

Research topic:
[topic]

Decision this research should support:
[decision]

Known constraints:
[constraints]

Your task:
1. Restate the research objective in one sentence.
2. Split it into 3-5 answerable subquestions.
3. Classify each subquestion as empirical comparison, mechanism explanation, scenario inquiry, or decision framing.
4. Identify missing scope constraints that would materially change the research method.
5. State assumptions explicitly.
6. Do not answer the underlying research question.
```

### 2. Hypothesis and rival-hypothesis prompt

```text
You are helping prepare a research plan. Do not answer the underlying question.

Research objective:
[objective]

Decision context:
[decision]

Your task:
1. Generate 3-7 hypotheses, mechanisms, or scenarios the research should test.
2. Include one skeptical or null hypothesis.
3. Include at least two rival explanations or counter-scenarios.
4. For each hypothesis, list what evidence would support it and what evidence would weaken it.
5. Separate facts, inferences, and speculation.
```

### 3. Source strategy prompt

```text
You are designing a source strategy for a research plan.

Research objective:
[objective]

Hypotheses to test:
[hypotheses]

Your task:
1. List the source categories needed to test each hypothesis.
2. Distinguish primary sources, secondary sources, databases, expert input, user evidence, and weak signals.
3. Suggest search queries and inclusion/exclusion criteria.
4. For each source category, state what it can prove, what it cannot prove, and likely biases.
5. Identify which sources are time-sensitive.
6. Do not provide a conclusion on the research question.
```

### 4. Evidence extraction prompt

```text
Extract evidence from the following source material.

Research objective:
[objective]

Source material:
[paste source]

Return a table with:
- claim
- direct observation or quoted support
- source/date
- evidence type
- relevance to the research question
- what this evidence supports
- what this evidence does not establish
- potential bias or limitation
- fact / inference / speculation label

Do not synthesize a final answer.
```

### 5. Adversarial critique prompt

```text
Act as an adversarial reviewer of this research plan.

Research objective:
[objective]

Proposed method:
[method]

Hypotheses:
[hypotheses]

Your task:
1. Identify the most fragile assumptions.
2. Identify likely selection bias, source bias, and confounding variables.
3. Generate at least 3 rival explanations or counter-scenarios.
4. State what evidence would most efficiently weaken or falsify the leading hypothesis.
5. Recommend whether to stop, continue, or escalate the research method.
6. Do not answer the underlying domain question directly.
```

### 6. Synthesis audit prompt

```text
Audit this draft research synthesis.

Research objective:
[objective]

Draft synthesis:
[paste draft]

Source log:
[paste source log]

Your task:
1. Identify claims that are not supported by the cited sources.
2. Identify where the draft confuses facts, inferences, and speculation.
3. Identify missing counterevidence or rival explanations.
4. Check whether the confidence level is too high, too low, or properly calibrated.
5. List what additional evidence would most improve the synthesis.
6. Do not rewrite the final conclusion unless asked.
```

## AI-assisted method output format

When including AI methods in a research pack, specify:
- tool role
- prompt to use
- expected output
- how the output enters the evidence table
- what must be independently verified
- failure modes
- escalation trigger
